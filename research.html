Government Affiliation and Rating Inequality: Uncovering the Corporate Credit Rating "Black Box"
Working Paper, 2025
SSRN Presentation (Finance II PhD, Fuqua)
Using 132,348 U.S. firm-quarter observations from 2000–2025, this paper studies how government customer relationships shape corporate credit ratings. Combining shock-interacted difference-in-differences with Debiased Machine Learning and Causal Forests, I find a genuine causal rating premium that operates at the extensive margin and is concentrated among investment-grade, low-leverage, high-margin firms. The premium amplifies after the GFC and partially reverts during COVID-19, while for speculative-grade firms government affiliation offers no buffering and reverses into a penalty during the pandemic—evidence that the rating "black box" encodes a time-varying, crisis-contingent signal that widens the gap across the investment-grade boundary.

The Limits of the Crowd: Trader Composition and the Boundary of Prediction-Market Efficiency
Work in Progress, 2026
Using 1.2 billion on-chain trades on Polymarket, this paper studies how trader composition and arbitrage capital determine prediction-market efficiency. I develop and structurally estimate a limits-of-arbitrage model, showing that liquidity attenuates the favorite–longshot bias while the profits from correcting mispricing concentrate among liquidity providers.
